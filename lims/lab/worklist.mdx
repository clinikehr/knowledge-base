<Note>
  **The Worklist is the drug-testing bench queue, and it reads drug-testing orders only.** A lab running standard clinical work will find it **empty** — and that is correct, not a fault. Clinical requests are worked from [Test requests](/lims/lab/test-requests) and the [Lab Reports](/lims/lab/reports) table.
</Note>

The **Worklist** puts every regulated specimen in one oldest-first list so the bench can see what is ageing, and lets you clear a run of negative oral-fluid screens in a single action.

## What you're looking at

The heading is **Worklist** and the subtitle states the job: "The bench queue, oldest first. Filter, select a batch, and mark received oral-fluid screens negative in one pass — urine and non-negatives go through the per-specimen forms."

Along the top, the status chips double as filters and each carries a live count in brackets — **All**, **Lab intake**, **Collected**, **In transit**, **Received**, **Screening**, **Confirmed**. Beside them are a search box (**Specimen ID, LAN or donor…**), a specimen-type select offering **All specimen types**, **Oral fluid** and **Urine**, the **List** | **Rack** view toggle, and a refresh button with no label on it.

The list columns are **Specimen**, **LAN**, **Donor**, **Type**, **Panel** (shown as **\{n\} analytes**), **Status** and **Received**, with a tick box on each row.

**The empty state.** When nothing matches you get **Nothing in the queue for these filters.** — widen the filters before you assume something is lost. A lab that runs no regulated collections sees that message on every filter, permanently.

## Switch between list and rack

**Rack** view lays the current filter out FIFO and colours each well by status, so an ageing specimen is visible from across the bench. A columns select appears next to the toggle in this view only, offering **5 / row**, **6 / row**, **8 / row**, **10 / row** and **12 / row**.

The caption is the thing to remember: "FIFO layout of the current filter — positions are a bench aid, not stored assignments. Click a well to select it for a batch action; double-click to open the specimen."

Nothing about a well is saved. Move the physical rack and the screen does not follow.

## Screen a batch negative

<Steps>
  <Step title="Filter to received oral fluid">
    Select the **Received** chip and set the specimen-type select to **Oral fluid**. Batch screening only ever applies to received oral-fluid specimens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the specimens">
    Tick the rows, or click the wells in rack view. The action bar reads **\{n\} selected**, and adds **· \{n\} eligible for batch screen (received oral-fluid only)** whenever your selection contains something that is not eligible. **Clear** drops the selection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the batch action">
    Select **Mark batch negative (\{n\})**. The dialog opens as **Mark \{n\} screens negative?** and explains what it will do: "Records an all-negative initial immunoassay screen for every selected received oral-fluid specimen and finalizes each as a negative result."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the technician">
    Enter the **Screening technician** — the field prompts for the "Name as it should appear on the chain of custody". This is the name that will be read back in a challenge, so enter the person who ran the specimens. The submit button stays disabled until you do.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the screens">
    Select **Record \{n\} negative screens**. It reads **Recording…** while it runs, then the toast reads **\{n\} specimens screened negative** and the specimens move on. Anything that failed is named individually so you can work it by hand.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  The dialog's own words are a compliance instruction, not a formality: "Only do this after the bench run confirmed every one of these specimens screened negative — any specimen with a non-negative must go through its own screen form instead." A batch attests to a negative screen for every specimen in it, under your named technician.
</Warning>

At most 100 specimens go in one batch — a larger selection returns **Batch too large — select at most 100 specimens**.

## Why urine is excluded

Batch negative is limited to oral fluid on purpose. Urine needs per-specimen validity testing — temperature, creatinine, pH, adulterants — and there is no honest way to attest to that in bulk, so urine is left out of the batch action by design. Work urine through the per-specimen screen form. See [Screening and confirmation](/lims/dot/screening-and-confirmation).

## Open a single specimen

Selecting a row opens the order workflow sheet with **Workflow** and **Chain of Custody** tabs. The **Workflow** tab shows whichever form the current status calls for — accession, screen, confirmation — so you never have to know which step is next. See [Accession an incoming specimen](/lims/lab/accessioning).

## Who can do this

Role decides whether **Worklist** opens. Permission decides what you may do on the specimens in it.

| Action | Who by default | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open **Worklist** and read the queue | Owner, Manager, **Lab Manager**, **Accessioning Clerk**, lab scientists, phlebotomists | Role → route |
| Accession a specimen from a row | **Accessioning Clerk**, **Lab Manager**, **Whole lab**, **All sites** | Permission — **Accession an incoming specimen** |
| Reject an unsuitable specimen | The same people | Permission — **Reject an unsuitable specimen** |
| Record a screen, including **Mark batch negative** | Anyone on **Own bench** and above | Permission — **Enter results at the bench** |
| Finalise the screen and decide how it is reported | **Whole lab**, **All sites** | Permission — **Verify and authorise results** |

The three access tiers do most of the work here. **Own bench** grants result entry only; **Whole lab** adds accessioning, rejection, verification and QC review at that site; **All sites** grants everything, everywhere. **A Lab Manager does not hold verify by default** — authorising a result is a scientist's clinical act, and it arrives with the *access tier*, not the administrative role.

A **phlebotomist** reaches test requests, the intake queue and patients, and nothing else in the lab. A **lab scientist** reaches the whole bench but is denied **Executive**, **Lab Analytics** and **Referral Commissions**.

Two behaviours surprise people:

- **Enforcement is opt-in, per person.** Somebody never saved in the permissions sheet is unrestricted, and the sheet warns **"Saving starts enforcing"** — from then on they can only do what is switched on there.
- **A denied action is recorded; an allowed one is not.** Where a figure is not yours to see it renders hidden or as a dash, never as a zero.

See [Permissions](/platform/team/permissions).

## Check it worked

- The specimens you batched have left the **Received** filter and the **Screening** count has moved by the same number.
- Each one's **Chain of Custody** tab shows an Initial Immunoassay Screen event naming the technician you entered.
- The counts on the status chips add up to the **All** count.

## Common issues

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The Worklist is empty">
    Expected, if you do not run drug testing. Regulated collections populate this queue; clinical requests live in [Test requests](/lims/lab/test-requests).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Mark batch negative is greyed out, or counts zero eligible">
    Your selection contains something other than a received oral-fluid specimen. Filter to **Received** and **Oral fluid** first, then reselect — the bar tells you how many of your selection qualify.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Batch too large — select at most 100 specimens">
    A batch is capped at 100. Split the run and record it in two passes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A few specimens failed inside the batch">
    Each failure is toasted with its specimen ID and reason, and the rest still went through. The usual cause is a specimen whose status moved while you were selecting. Open those on their own.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A specimen screened non-negative">
    Take it out of the batch and open it on its own. A non-negative goes to confirmation, never through the batch action — see [Screening and confirmation](/lims/dot/screening-and-confirmation).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Everything on the bench is disabled">
    You are at a collection centre, which shows **This site is a collection center — register specimens and dispatch them to your laboratory. Accessioning, screening and reporting are done at the full lab and are disabled here.**
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I entered the wrong screening technician">
    Ask a Lab Manager. The name is written to the chain of custody, so it is corrected deliberately and the correction is itself recorded — see [Audit log](/platform/security/audit-log).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why is my Worklist empty?">
    Because it lists drug-testing orders and nothing else. It is not a fault, a filter problem or a permission problem — a lab doing only clinical chemistry will never see a row here. Work those from [Test requests](/lims/lab/test-requests).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do rack positions match my physical rack?">
    No, and they are not meant to. Rack view is a FIFO drawing of the current filter — "positions are a bench aid, not stored assignments" — so nothing you do on screen moves a tube, and nothing you do at the bench moves a well.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I batch urine if I ran validity testing on every one?">
    No. The action only ever accepts received oral-fluid specimens, by design. Urine goes through the per-specimen screen form so the validity results are recorded against the specimen they belong to.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does a batch negative finish the specimen?">
    It records an all-negative initial immunoassay screen and finalises each one as a negative result, so those specimens leave the bench. Anything non-negative is untouched by it and still needs confirmation before any analyte can be called **Positive**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I work offline from this queue?">
    Order capture goes offline; the bench does not. Accessioning, screening, confirmation, result entry and verification are status-guarded steps whose conflicts need a person at the bench, so they stay online — see [Working offline](/platform/apps/offline).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
