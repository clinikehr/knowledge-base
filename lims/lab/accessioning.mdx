Accessioning is the moment a specimen stops being someone else's problem and becomes yours. You give it an accession number, record when it arrived and in what condition, and the handoff is written to the chain of custody.

## Before you start: is this site a full lab?

A site registered as a collection centre cannot accession. Its bench screens carry a banner reading **This site is a collection center — register specimens and dispatch them to your laboratory. Accessioning, screening and reporting are done at the full lab and are disabled here.** Push past it and the action returns **This site is a collection center — accession, screening and reporting happen at your full laboratory**.

That is deliberate. A collection centre draws and refers; the full lab receives and reports. The site kind is chosen when the workspace is created — **Full laboratory — testing on site** or **Collection center — samples drawn and referred to your lab** — and it is not a per-user setting. If your banner is wrong, [contact support](/platform/help/contact-support).

## What you're looking at

You reach the form from the [Worklist](/lims/lab/worklist): select the specimen's row and the order workflow sheet opens with **Workflow** and **Chain of Custody** tabs. The **Workflow** tab shows whichever form the current status calls for, so an **In transit** or **Lab intake** specimen shows the **Laboratory Accession** card.

| Field | What it wants |
| --- | --- |
| **Accession Number \*** | "Must be unique. This is the lab's internal identifier for this specimen." Shaped like `LAB-2024-00001` |
| **Received Date & Time \*** | "Pick received date & time" — the moment the specimen actually arrived |
| **Primary Specimen Seal (Bottle A) \*** | **Yes — Seal Intact** or **No — Seal Broken** |
| **Split Specimen Seal (Bottle B) \*** | **Yes — Intact** or **No — Broken** |
| **Seal Condition Notes \*** | Appears once the primary seal is marked broken — "Describe the condition of the seal in detail." |
| **Bottle B Storage Location** | "Where the split specimen is stored (freezer ID, rack, position, etc.)" — for example "Freezer A-2, Rack 3, Position 7" |

The submit button changes with the answer you give: **Record Accession** normally, **Reject Specimen** the moment the primary seal is marked broken.

## Accession the specimen

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the specimen">
    On the **Worklist**, find it — filter by a status chip or search by **Specimen ID, LAN or donor…** — and select the row.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the accession number and time">
    Fill in **Accession Number \*** and **Received Date & Time \***. Record the time the specimen actually arrived, not the time you got to the keyboard; it is the clock every turnaround measure runs from, and a keyboard time flatters your numbers. See [Turnaround time](/lims/lab/turnaround-time).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check both seals">
    Answer **Primary Specimen Seal (Bottle A) \*** and **Split Specimen Seal (Bottle B) \*** against the paperwork, inspecting the bottles rather than the shipping box.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe any damage">
    Marking the primary seal broken reveals **Seal Condition Notes \***. Describe what you saw — the seal was torn, the signature did not match, the bottle leaked. This note is what a reviewer reads months later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record where Bottle B is stored">
    Fill in **Bottle B Storage Location** in enough detail that a colleague can retrieve the split without you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the accession">
    Select **Record Accession**. The toast reads **Lab accession recorded**, the specimen moves on to the next stage of the queue, and a custody event is written with the time and your name.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## A broken seal is a fatal flaw

Mark the primary seal broken and the form says so before you go any further: **Fatal Flaw — Broken Seal** — "A broken tamper-evident seal is a fatal flaw per 49 CFR § 40.83. This specimen will be rejected for testing and cannot be used for any result determination."

Selecting **Reject Specimen** then raises **Confirm Specimen Rejection**: "You are about to reject this specimen for testing due to a broken seal. This action will advance the order status to "Rejected for Testing" and cannot be undone. The donor may need to be retested." Choose **Confirm Rejection** and the toast reads **Specimen rejected for testing due to broken seal**.

<Warning>
  Rejection cannot be undone, and it is the correct outcome. Do not click back through the form to make a broken seal go away so the queue clears — a compromised specimen produces a result nobody can stand behind, and the donor is recollected instead.
</Warning>

## Rejecting for other reasons

Rejecting is a separate grant from accessioning, and the two together are the Accessioning Clerk's whole job. Reject when the specimen cannot produce a defensible result: wrong tube, insufficient volume, no label, a seal you cannot vouch for, a transport delay past the analyte's stability. The rejection is recorded as its own custody event, so the reason survives.

## Reading the chain of custody

The **Chain of Custody** tab groups every event into four phases — **Specimen Intake**, **Laboratory Analysis**, **Reporting** and **Post-Report** — each headed with its own event count. Every event card shows a **Seal Intact** or **Seal Broken** pill, the released-by → received-by handoff, any notes, the timestamp and the elapsed time since the previous event.

Events you will see include Order Created, CCF Completed, Specimen Collected, Specimen Shipped, Specimen Received at Lab, Specimen Rejected, Initial Immunoassay Screen, GC-MS / LC-MS-MS Confirmation, Lab Report Finalized, Report Sent to MRO, MRO Verified, Reported to DER, Order Cancelled, Split Retest Requested, Split Retest Completed and Custody Transfer.

## Who can do this

Role decides whether the bench opens, and — for this one form — role also decides whether you may book a specimen in. Permission takes over from the screen onward.

| Action | Who by default | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open the **Worklist** and read a specimen | Owner, Manager, **Lab Manager**, **Accessioning Clerk**, lab scientists, phlebotomists | Role → route |
| Accession an incoming specimen | Owner, Manager, Doctor | Role — the form checks the job title |
| Reject an unsuitable specimen (a broken seal, on the same form) | The same people | Role — the same check |
| Record the screen that follows | Anyone on **Own bench** and above | Permission — **Enter results at the bench** |
| Finalise the screen and decide how it is reported | **Whole lab**, **All sites** | Permission — **Verify and authorise results** |

**The two specimen permissions are not enforced on this form.** **Accession an incoming specimen** and **Reject an unsuitable specimen** exist in the Diagnostics catalogue and the permissions sheet offers them, but the accession form does not read either one — it checks the person's job-title role instead, and accepts Owner, Manager and Doctor. Granting an Accessioning Clerk the specimen toggles does not by itself let them work this form; their **job title** is what decides. Rejection is not a separate act at all — it is the broken-seal branch of the same accession form.

From the screen onward the permissions are real: **Enter results at the bench**, then **Verify and authorise results**. A **Lab Manager** holds neither by default — authorising a result is a scientist's clinical act, and it arrives with the *access tier* rather than the administrative role. A **QC Officer** is review-only by design.

Two behaviours surprise people:

- **Enforcement is opt-in, per person.** Somebody never saved in the permissions sheet is unrestricted, and the sheet warns **"Saving starts enforcing"** — from then on they can only do what is switched on there.
- **A denied action is recorded; an allowed one is not.** A figure you are not entitled to see is hidden or rendered as a dash, never as a zero.

See [Permissions](/platform/team/permissions).

## Check it worked

- The specimen carries its accession number and has moved to the **Received** filter on the **Worklist**.
- The **Chain of Custody** tab shows a new event under **Specimen Intake** with your name, the received time and the correct seal pill.
- The elapsed time on the new event is measured from the previous handoff, not from now.

## Common issues

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The accession form is not on the Workflow tab">
    The tab shows the form for the specimen's current status. If it is still **Collected** or **In transit**, the arrival has not been recorded yet; if it is already **Screening**, accessioning is done.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Everything on the bench is disabled">
    You are at a collection centre. Register and dispatch the specimen instead — accessioning happens at the full lab.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I cannot save without seal notes">
    **Seal Condition Notes \*** appears as soon as the primary seal is marked broken, and it is there to be filled in. Describe the damage rather than looking for a way round it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The button now says Reject Specimen and I did not want that">
    You answered **No — Seal Broken** on **Primary Specimen Seal (Bottle A)**. If that was a mis-tap, correct the answer before submitting and the button returns to **Record Accession**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Failed to record accession">
    The save did not go through. Check the accession number is not already in use — it must be unique — and try again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I used the wrong accession number">
    Ask a Lab Manager. Identifiers on an accessioned specimen are part of the custody record, so corrections are made deliberately and leave their own trail — see [Audit log](/platform/security/audit-log).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Worklist is empty">
    It is the drug-testing bench queue. A lab running only standard clinical work sees nothing there, correctly — work those from [Test requests](/lims/lab/test-requests).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens if a seal is broken on arrival?">
    The form names it as a fatal flaw under **49 CFR § 40.83**, and the specimen is rejected for testing rather than accessioned. It cannot be used for any result determination, the rejection cannot be undone, and the donor may need to be recollected.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why does Bottle B matter if we only test Bottle A?">
    Because a donor may request a split retest of Bottle B after a confirmed positive. Recording where it is stored is what makes that possible months later — and the storage note is the only thing standing between a request and a search of the freezer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Should I use the time on the courier's paperwork or the time I keyed it in?">
    The time the specimen arrived. Turnaround is measured from it, so a keyboard time makes your lab look slower or faster than it is and misstates the custody record.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I accession offline?">
    No. Order capture goes offline; accessioning does not. It is a status-guarded step whose conflicts need a person at the bench — see [Working offline](/platform/apps/offline).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does accessioning a specimen report anything to anyone?">
    No. It only books the specimen in. Screening, confirmation and the report are separate steps, and no analyte can be called **Positive** until confirmation — see [Screening and confirmation](/lims/dot/screening-and-confirmation).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
