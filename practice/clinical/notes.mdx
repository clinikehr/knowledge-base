**Notes** is where a solo or team practice does all of its clinical documentation. You write about a client, save it as a draft while you think, then sign it — and signing is what turns it into a record.

## What you're looking at

The left rail is headed **Clinical Notes** and holds **New Note**, **Templates**, **Custom Templates** and **Snippets**, with an **AI** heading below them over a single entry, **AI Notes**. That last one opens a panel over whatever you are doing rather than navigating away.

**New Note** opens a page headed **New Progress Note** — "Create a new clinical note or documentation entry" — offering three ways in:

| Card | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Use Template** | "Start from a template" — the structure is already decided |
| **Blank Note** | "Start from scratch" |
| **AI Note Taker** | "AI-powered note generation" — from a recording or a document |

Below those: a **Waiting on you (\{n\})** card, shown only when something is pending your countersignature; the **Quick Note** card you write in; and **Recent Notes**, which has two tabs. **All Notes** is a searchable list badged **Locked**, **Draft** or **Final** with a **View** button on each row — the search waits for you to pause typing, and the list stays on screen while it updates. **AI Notes Draft** lists drafts written by an assistant or through the API; see [Review a draft the assistant wrote](/practice/clinical/note-assistant#ai-notes-draft). A brand-new practice sees "No recent notes found. Create your first note above." and no **Waiting on you** card at all.

## Write a quick note

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name it and pick a shape">
    Enter a **Note Title** ("Enter note title..."), then a **Template Type**: **SOAP Note**, **Progress Note**, **Initial Assessment**, **Therapy Session**, **Discharge Summary**, **Treatment Plan** or **General Note**. A note with the **Discharge Summary** type is the discharge summary for a client leaving your care as an outpatient. If your clinic requires your signed notes to be co-signed, it is sent to your supervisor like any other note, and it follows the discharge-summary settings in [Co-sign settings](/practice/clinical/cosign-settings).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach it to a client">
    Open the **Client** picker ("Search for a client...") and search by name, client ID or national ID. If they are not registered, **Register new client** takes you there without losing what you have typed.

    A draft saves without a client. A final note does not — you are stopped with "Please select a client before saving the final note".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag it and write">
    **Tags** ("Add a tag...") make the note findable later. Write the body in **Note Content**. The placeholder tells you the shortcut that matters: "Start typing your note content... Type / for snippets".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save as a draft, or sign it">
    **Save as Draft** keeps it editable. **Sign & Lock** finishes it. **Upload File** attaches supporting material, and **Clear** empties the card.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Add the visit details

A saved note has a **Visit details** button in its toolbar, beside the note assistant. A small dot on the button means some details are already recorded. The details record the **Type of visit** (**Ambulatory**, **Emergency**, **Inpatient**, **Virtual** or **Home health**), when the visit **Started** and **Ended**, and a short **Reason for the visit**. These are facts about the visit, not part of what the note says, and none of them is required.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the details">
    On a draft, select **Add visit details**, or **Edit visit details** if some are already there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in what you know">
    Choose the **Type of visit**, pick **Started** and **Ended** from the date and time pickers, and write the **Reason for the visit** in up to 500 characters. Select **Clear start** or **Clear end** to take a time out again. The end cannot be before the start.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Select **Save visit details**. You see "Visit details saved", and they are still there when you leave the note and come back.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A signed note keeps its visit details as they were: its button reads **Visit details** and shows them without letting you change them, with a line saying why. A signed note with no visit details has no button. You can change the details of any draft you are allowed to edit, including one a connected app opened. If you cannot change a colleague's note, you cannot change its visit details either; the message tells you whose note it is.

## Sign and lock

Select **Sign & Lock** to open the **Sign & Lock Note** sheet — "Once signed, this note becomes read-only". Check the **Signing Provider**, then add the **Provider Signature**: **Typed**, which previews under the heading **Electronic Signature**, or **Draw**, which needs a real signature drawn in the box before it will accept.

Below that is an attestation you must tick — it confirms you have reviewed the note, that locking it prevents further changes, and that "This electronic signature has the same legal effect as a handwritten signature." Then select **Sign & Lock**. You will see "Note Signed & Locked".

A locked note carries a **Read-Only (Locked)** badge and reads "This note is locked and cannot be edited" where its buttons used to be. Both of those are selectable — they reopen the sheet in unlock mode, where you confirm and select **Unlock Note**. The note becomes editable again and must be re-signed.

## Countersign a colleague's note

<Availability editions={['clinic']} plans="Team practices where a clinician has a named supervisor" roles="The supervisor named on the clinician's profile, and the clinician" />

When a clinician who has a supervisor signs a note, it goes to the supervisor and is **not final** until they countersign it. Nothing changes for a clinician with no supervisor.

### Where a note waiting for you shows up

- **Notes** in the sidebar carries a number: the notes waiting for your signature plus your own notes that were sent back. It is just a count — it never names a person or a client.
- The **Dashboard** has a **Notes that need you** card with **Awaiting my signature** (oldest first) and **Changes requested**. Select a row to open it. The card does not appear when nothing is waiting.
- The **Co-sign inbox** on the Notes page is where you work through them. It appears only when something is waiting for you or you have submitted notes.
- On a client's chart, the **Notes** tab shows **Pending co-sign**, **Changes requested** or **Co-signed** beside each note, with **Review & sign** for the supervisor and **See comments** for the author.
- **Care team → My Patients** marks a client **Needs my signature**, has a **Needs my signature** filter, and the client's detail sheet shows the count.
- You also get a notification when a note arrives, when one you returned is resubmitted, and when yours is countersigned or sent back.

### The co-sign inbox

**For me to co-sign** has four tabs: **To co-sign**, **Overdue**, **Sent back** (notes you returned that are waiting on the author) and **Done (30 days)**. The oldest note comes first, and you can filter by associate. Each row shows the kind of note, the **client's initials** (or **Restricted** if you are not allowed to see that client), the associate, how long it has waited, and a deadline. The deadline is counted in your practice's open days; it shows **—** for a kind of note your practice has set to have no deadline. See [Co-signing: statements, deadlines and cover](/practice/clinical/cosign-settings).

**My submitted notes** is the same for an associate: **Waiting**, **Changes requested** and **Co-signed**. **Changes requested** counts in your sidebar number and your notifications.

For a treatment plan review or a measure interpretation, the inbox row and the notification open that exact record in the client's chart instead of the Notes page. If you may not see the client, the link falls back to the Notes page.

Select **Open** on a row to read the note's sections and co-sign it or ask for changes from the same panel. To co-sign several at once, **Open** each note you want, tick them, and select **Co-sign selected**. One signature covers the batch. Only notes you opened in the last 12 hours can be ticked, and if any note in the batch has stopped being eligible nothing is signed and you are told.

Owners and managers also see a **Practice overview**: everything waiting in the practice grouped **By supervisor** or **By associate**, with how long each has waited. **Move to…** hands a waiting note to another of the author's supervisors — only one who is allowed to see that client. A request about a restricted client or a sensitive note you are not allowed to open is counted but not shown.

### Countersign it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read it">
    Select **Open** (or **Read the note**) and read the note properly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose what you attest to">
    Under **I attest that**, pick one of your practice's statements. The one your practice prefers for this kind of note is already chosen. You can add a sentence of your own. If your licence on file has expired or is missing, the panel says so and **Co-sign** stays off until you update it in your profile.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Countersign">
    Select **Co-sign** in the panel and sign. The note is then final, and the statement you chose is printed under your name on the note and its PDF.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Ask for changes instead

Select **Request changes**. A panel opens with the note's sections:

- Write **why you are sending it back**. With only this, the note goes back as a plain return.
- Select **Comment** on a section to leave a comment about just that section.
- Select **Edit** on a plain-text section to reword it. Your wording is recorded as a suggestion — who, what it said before and what you propose — and **nothing is written into the author's note until the author accepts it**. You cannot countersign while you have suggestions waiting; send the note back, or select **Discard my edit**.

Select **Send back to author**. The note becomes editable again for its author. If the author had already signed it, that signature is kept in the note's history as superseded, and the author signs the note again when they resubmit — so the final note carries both signatures on the same text.

You can send a note back as many times as it needs. Every round stays on the note, and each time it comes back you see which sections changed since your last review.

### If your note comes back

The panel on the note reads **Changes requested**, with your supervisor's comments grouped by section.

1. Select **Mark resolved** on each comment as you deal with it. Comments are a checklist; they never stop you resubmitting.
2. For each suggested edit, select **Accept** (the wording goes into your note — the page reloads to show it) or **Reject**. A rejection needs a reason, which your supervisor reads. If you changed that section yourself since, the edit cannot be accepted; reject it, or copy the wording by hand. **You cannot resubmit until every suggested edit is accepted or rejected.**
3. Make your own changes, then sign the note again. That resubmits it.

Your supervisor then sees **Since your review**, a list of the sections that changed (section names only), and which sections they commented on that you did not touch.

### If you rejected your supervisor's edit

When the note comes back to the supervisor they see your reason and can **Withdraw my edit**, select **Request changes** to discuss it, or **Co-sign with noted disagreement**. A plain **Co-sign** is not offered while a rejected edit is unanswered. A note signed that way reads **Co-signed with noted disagreement**, and the disagreement stays in the history.

### Who can read the comments

Review comments, suggested edits and the earlier signatures are **internal**. They are never printed on the note's PDF, never shown in the client portal, and never included in a record export, a release of records or the EHR API. The author, the supervisors who can act on the note, and the practice's owners and managers can read them in the note's history. For a sensitive note only the author, the named supervisor and the practice owner can.

### Sensitive notes and restricted clients

- A note marked **sensitive** goes to **one named supervisor** — the first of your supervisors who is able to see the client. Only that supervisor can open, comment on, suggest edits to and countersign it, and only while it is waiting. Nobody else gains access, and every time they open it is recorded.
- If the client is **restricted**, the note goes to a supervisor who is allowed to see that client. If none of your supervisors can, you are told before anything is sent: *None of your supervisors can see this patient. Ask an owner or manager to add one to the patient's allowed staff.* Access is never widened for you.

### Billing

If you create or send an insurance or EAP claim for a client whose note is still waiting for a countersignature, or was sent back, the claim screen shows **Note not co-signed yet**. It is a warning only — you can still send the claim.

## Mark a note sensitive

<Availability plans="Essential and up" roles="The author, the client's care team, or the clinic owner" />

For a note that needs extra discretion — behavioural health, substance use, reproductive health, HIV status, and similar — select the lock icon beside it in the notes list, choose a category, and select **Save**. Only the author, the client's care team, and the clinic owner can open it afterwards, or change the mark — a manager cannot, even though a manager can open most other clinical records; everyone else sees **Sensitive note** in the list, with no title or content. This applies even when the client themself is not restricted. Choose **Not sensitive** in the same dialog to remove the mark. Full detail, including what "care team" means here, is in [Restrict a client to assigned staff → Sensitive notes and documents](/practice/work/client-security#sensitive-notes-and-documents).

## The AI writing tools

Two live in the editor toolbar and work on text you have already written:

- **AI Rewrite** improves a passage — "Content has been enhanced. Review before saving."
- **AI Autocomplete** suggests the next few words in grey as you type. Its tooltip reads "AI Autocomplete (on) — Tab to accept". Press Tab to take it, Escape to dismiss it.

Both need Essential or above — they are switched off on Free, not held back for a higher tier. On a paid plan they use agent credits: 1 for each AI Rewrite and 0.35 for each AI Autocomplete suggestion. If the balance runs out, suggestions stop for the rest of your session and you see one message, with **Buy credits** for the owner and managers. You can keep writing by hand. See [AI credits](/platform/plans/credits). To draft a whole note from a recording instead, use [the AI Note Taker](/practice/clinical/ai-notes) — a separate add-on, not a plan feature.

## Who can do this

Role decides whether **Notes** opens. Permission decides what you may do once it is open.

| Action | Who can do it by default | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Write and save a draft | Any clinical staff member | Role |
| Attach a note to a client you do not own | Owner, manager, and staff set to **Full client list** or **Entire practice** | The access tier chosen when they were invited |
| Sign and lock a note | The signing clinician | Role — plus the Essential plan or above |
| Countersign somebody else's note | The colleague named as their supervisor | The supervisor relationship set when they were invited |
| Build or edit a template | Owner, manager | Permission — **Edit note and form templates** |

Two behaviours surprise people:

- **Enforcement is opt-in, per person.** A colleague never saved in the permissions sheet is unrestricted. The sheet warns **"Saving starts enforcing"**.
- **A denied action is recorded; an allowed one is not.** Hiding **Notes** from somebody's sidebar is a convenience, not a security boundary.

See [Staff permissions](/platform/team/permissions).

## Check it worked

The note is in **Recent Notes** with the right client against it and a **Final** or **Locked** badge, and it is on that client's chart. A signed note names the signing provider and cannot be edited until it is unlocked.

## Common issues

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="This note was written on one of your clinic's forms, so it opens in the client's record rather than here">
    You opened a note written on a **note form** from somewhere other than the client's record — for example **View** under **Recent Notes**. Nothing has been lost: every answer is still on the note. Open the client, go to **Notes**, and select the note there to read or continue it. See [Open a note written on a note form](/practice/clinical/note-templates#open-a-note-written-on-a-note-form).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Please select a client before saving the final note">
    A final note must belong to somebody. Choose them in the **Client** picker — or, if they are not registered yet, use **Register new client** in the picker itself. See [Clients](/practice/work/clients).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="eSignatures & Locking Limit Reached">
    Signing and locking need the Essential plan or above: "The ability to electronically sign and lock clinical notes is a premium compliance feature." On Free you can write and save drafts. See [Change your plan](/platform/plans/change-plan).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Limit Reached when saving a note">
    The Free plan caps how many notes you can write each month. The count resets at the start of the next month; paid plans remove the cap.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Sign & Lock is greyed out">
    No client is selected. The button stays disabled until one is.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="AI Rewrite or AI Autocomplete shows a flag and will not run">
    Your practice is on Free — both need Essential or above, and the tooltip reads "Upgrade to use AI Rewrite". They are separate from the AI Note Taker, which is included for every clinician on Essential, Team and Enterprise (not on Free).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A locked note needs correcting">
    Select the **Read-Only (Locked)** badge, confirm, and select **Unlock Note**. Unlocking removes the signature, so re-sign after editing. Both the lock and the unlock are in your [audit log](/platform/security/audit-log) — amend rather than working around it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I edit a signed note?">
    Not while it is locked. Unlock it, edit, and sign again. The original signature is removed by unlocking, and both events are recorded, so the history of the correction is preserved.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does a draft show on the client's chart?">
    A draft is visible to your practice as a draft, and can exist without a client attached at all. Only a saved final note reads as documentation of the encounter.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between Template Type here and a template?">
    **Template Type** is a label on a quick note. A **template** is a whole structured form with its own sections — see [Note templates](/practice/clinical/note-templates).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I get a snippet into a note?">
    Type **/** in the note body and pick it from the menu. The **Snippets** page's own buttons copy to the clipboard instead — see [Snippets](/practice/clinical/snippets).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I write a note during a video call?">
    Yes — telehealth has its own notes panel beside the call. See [Run a video session](/practice/clinical/telehealth).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
