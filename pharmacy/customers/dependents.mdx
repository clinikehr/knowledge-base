Someone is at the counter collecting for a regular customer. Is that expected? The **People** tab on a customer's profile is the answer: a short list of who that customer has said may pick up for them, with the relationship and anything your counter should check.

## What you're looking at

**People** is the fourth tab on a customer's profile, between **Account** and **Insights**. It is a plain list, one card per person, and a single button above it: **Add someone who may collect**.

A pharmacy that has never added anyone sees **No dependents** — "Add a family member or errand runner allowed to collect."

Each card carries:

| On the card | What it tells the counter |
| --- | --- |
| The name, in bold | Who to expect |
| The relationship and phone underneath | How they are connected, and who to ring |
| Any notes you wrote | The condition on the collection — read this before handing anything over |
| An **Own profile** badge | This person also shops with you in their own right, and that record is named |
| An **Inactive** badge, dimmed | They were authorised once and are not now |
| **Edit** | The only button on the card — everything else happens in the dialog |

**Add someone who may collect** opens **Add dependent** — "Someone allowed to collect products on this customer's behalf." — with four fields: **Full name**, **Phone**, **Relationship** and **Notes**. Only the first is required, and the confirm button reads **Add**. Selecting **Edit** on a card reopens the same dialog headed **Edit dependent**, where the button reads **Save** and a red **Deactivate** appears in the bottom-left corner. **Deactivate** is not on the card — you get to it through **Edit**.

## Add someone who may collect

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the People tab">
    Find the customer under **Customers**, open their profile, and select **People**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Add someone who may collect">
    The **Add dependent** dialog opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter who they are">
    **Full name** is the only required field; without it you get **"A dependent needs a name."**

    **Phone** is optional but worth having — it is what lets your counter ring the person who is collecting rather than the customer themself when something is unclear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say how they are connected">
    **Relationship** takes a free-text description — "e.g. Daughter, Driver". Write what your staff would actually say out loud, because this is the line they read at the counter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add anything the counter should check">
    **Notes** — "Anything the counter should check". This is the field that earns the feature its keep: "collects the monthly repeat only", "must show her own ID", "not authorised for controlled items".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add them">
    Select **Add**; the button reads **Saving…** while it commits. You get **"Dependent added"**, and the person appears in the list with their relationship and phone. On an existing person the same button reads **Save** and confirms **"Dependent updated"**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Using the list at the counter

Dependents are a **record, not a rule**. Nothing in the till blocks a sale to someone who is not on the list — the judgement stays with your staff, which is where it belongs. What the list gives them is a fast, unambiguous check instead of a phone call:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the customer">
    Search **Customers** by name, phone or ID number. See [Customer records](/pharmacy/customers/index).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open People">
    Read the name, the relationship and the notes. The notes are the part that changes a decision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hand over, or don't">
    An **Inactive** badge means this person was authorised once and is not now. Treat it as a "no", not as an out-of-date entry.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A dependent is not a login. They cannot see the customer's records, and adding them grants no access to anything — it only records that the customer said they may collect.
</Note>

## Deactivate — do not delete

When someone should no longer collect, select **Edit** on their card and then **Deactivate** at the bottom left of the dialog. Confirm on the **Deactivate dependent** dialog, which spells out what happens:

> \{Name\} will no longer be listed as allowed to collect. The record is kept, so who was authorised and when can still be answered.

That last sentence is the whole reason deactivation exists. If a collection is ever questioned — the wrong person picked up, a family disagreement, a supply query — the useful answer is _who was on the list at the time_, and a deleted record cannot give it. A deactivated person stays visible, dimmed, with an **Inactive** badge, and can be put back on the list by editing them and saving. You get **"Dependent deactivated"** on the way out and **"Dependent updated"** on the way back.

## Who can do this

**Role** decides whether **Customers** opens at all, and that is the only gate on this tab. A **Cashier / Till Operator** reaches **POS** and **Sales** and nothing else, so the person most likely to be standing at the counter when a dependent turns up cannot open the list themselves. Plan for that: either the check happens on a manager's screen, or the till role is the wrong one for that colleague.

| Action | Who by default | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open **People** and read the list | Anyone who can open **Customers** | Role — a cashier cannot reach it |
| Add someone with **Add dependent** | Anyone who can open **Customers** | Role — not a grantable permission |
| **Edit** a dependent's details or notes | Anyone who can open **Customers** | Role — not a grantable permission |
| **Deactivate** someone, or put them back | Anyone who can open **Customers** | Role — not a grantable permission |
| Hand over a controlled item to whoever collects | Dispensing Supervisor | **Witness a controlled-drug dispense** |
| Sell to them at the counter | Anyone whose role reaches **POS** | Role — selling is not permission-gated |

There is no separate permission for the list itself, and nothing here gates a figure — a dependent record holds no money, so nothing on this tab ever renders as **"Hidden — needs authorisation from your clinic owner."**

Two behaviours surprise people:

- **Enforcement is opt-in, per person.** A colleague never saved in the permissions sheet is unrestricted, and the sheet warns **"Saving starts enforcing"**.
- **A denied action is recorded; an allowed one is not.** Hiding **Customers** from a sidebar is a convenience, not a security boundary.

See [Staff permissions](/platform/team/permissions).

## Check it worked

- The **People** tab lists the person, with their relationship and phone underneath the name.
- If the dependent is themselves a registered customer, their row carries an **Own profile** badge naming that record.
- **Edit** on the row reopens the same dialog for changes.
- After a deactivation the row is still there, dimmed, with an **Inactive** badge.

## Common issues

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="'A dependent needs a name.'">
    **Full name** is required. Everything else on the dialog is optional — though a phone number and a relationship are what make the entry useful at the counter.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'Could not save'">
    The dependent was not added or updated and the list is unchanged. Reopen **People** to check before entering the person a second time.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'Could not deactivate'">
    The person is still active and still listed as allowed to collect. Try again, and if it persists, add a note on the record so the counter has the instruction in the meantime.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I cannot find the Deactivate button">
    It is inside the dialog, not on the card: select **Edit**, and **Deactivate** sits at the bottom left. It also shows only on someone currently active — a person already deactivated carries an **Inactive** badge instead, and editing them and saving puts them back on the list.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The dependent also shops with us in their own right">
    That is fine — they can have their own customer record as well. Where the two are linked, the dependent's row shows an **Own profile** badge. Their own purchases, debts and money on account stay entirely separate from the customer they collect for.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I cannot open the Customers screen at all">
    A cashier reaches only **POS** and **Sales**. Ask an owner or manager to check your role — no permission toggle re-opens this screen for a till role.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can someone collect for a customer who isn't listed?">
    Yes. Nothing at the till checks this list or blocks the sale — it is a record your staff read, not a rule the system enforces. The judgement stays with the person at the counter, and the list is there to make that judgement quick.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does adding a dependent let them see the customer's records?">
    No. A dependent is not a login and grants no access to anything. It records only that the customer said this person may collect for them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How many people can I add?">
    As many as the customer names. In practice the list is most useful when it is short and specific — three named people with clear notes beats a dozen entries nobody reads.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Should I delete someone who has moved away?">
    Deactivate them instead. A deleted record cannot answer "who was authorised at the time", which is the exact question that gets asked when a collection is queried months later.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a dependent collect a controlled item?">
    That is a dispensing decision, not a list decision. Use **Notes** to record the customer's own instruction — "not authorised for controlled items" is a common one — and let the person holding **Witness a controlled-drug dispense** make the call at the counter.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
