Import loads products into your catalogue from a spreadsheet. Export takes your catalogue back out as Excel or CSV. Both sit on the **Inventory** toolbar, and neither one touches the quantity on your shelves.

## The two things people get wrong

Read these before you build a file. Almost every question about import and export is one of them.

<Note>
  **Import creates catalogue rows only.** A row becomes a product, not a quantity on a shelf. The sheet says so itself: "Each row creates one catalog item — stock is added afterwards through Receive". Quantities arrive through **Receive**, or in bulk through the separate **Opening stock** button — see [Stock control](/pharmacy/inventory/index).

  **Export deliberately leaves on-hand quantities out** — "Stock on hand is not included — quantities are managed through Receive and Dispense, so re-importing this file never overwrites your live stock."
</Note>

Together those mean one thing worth saying plainly: your stock levels live in ClinikEHR and nowhere else. A spreadsheet is a way to load a catalogue and a way to read one, never a way to set what you hold.

## What you're looking at

Neither is a page — each opens a sheet over the screen you are on.

**Import** opens **Bulk-import items**, badged **Catalog Import**. Under **Start from the template** sits **Download sample CSV** — "Download the sample CSV to see the required headers, expected formats, and two example rows." Below that, **Upload your file** is a **Click to upload or drag and drop** target, constrained to "**.csv, .xlsx or .xls — max 5MB, up to 1000 rows**". While it reads, the sheet says **Reading spreadsheet…**; afterwards **Replace file** swaps the upload.

The **Preview** that follows is the part worth slowing down for. Three badges count what it found — **\{n\} new**, **\{n\} to update** and **\{n\} need fixing** — and each row is labelled either **new** or "updates existing · matched on upc" (or `sku`). A row that changes nothing says so: "No changes — this row matches what is already stored." A **Field reference** below lists every column with its hint, and required ones carry a star.

**Export** opens **Export inventory catalog**, badged **Catalog Export**. It shows "\{n\} item(s) ready to export", a **File format** choice — **Excel (.xlsx)**, "Formatted workbook — opens natively in Excel.", the default, or **CSV (.csv)**, "Plain text — universal, opens anywhere." — and the full **Columns included** list. The footnote reads "The export is logged against the audit trail."

**An empty catalogue** can still open both. Export simply refuses, with the button reading **No items to export** rather than handing you a header row and nothing under it.

## Import is an update as well as an insert

This is the fact the word "import" hides. Every row is matched against your catalogue on its **barcode first, then its SKU**:

- **A match** updates that product. Columns your sheet leaves out are left untouched.
- **No match** creates a new product.

So a corrected price list is a legitimate re-import, not a duplicate-maker — provided your barcodes and SKUs are right. Where they are missing or wrong, the same product arrives twice under two names.

## Import your catalogue

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the importer">
    Select **Import** on the **Inventory** toolbar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start from the template">
    Select **Download sample CSV**; a toast confirms **"Sample template downloaded"**. Fill your products into that file rather than inventing column names — the headers are what the importer matches on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload the file">
    Select **Click to upload or drag and drop**. Only `.csv`, `.xlsx` and `.xls` are accepted, up to 5 MB.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the preview before you commit">
    Check the **\{n\} new** and **\{n\} to update** counts against what you expected. A file you meant as new products showing mostly "to update" means it is matching things you did not intend to change — stop and check your barcode column.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import">
    The button names the outcome — **Add \{n\}, update \{n\}** — then reports **Submitted**, **Created**, **Updated** and **Failed**. Anything that failed comes back through **Download failed rows with error column**: fix that file and re-upload only it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  If you upload a sales report exported from an old till system, the importer recognises it — "POS report recognised and converted" — strips duplicate and subtotal rows, and numbers repeated price variants "#2" for you to rename. It still imports no quantities.
</Tip>

## Export your catalogue

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the exporter">
    Select **Export** on the toolbar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the file format">
    **Excel (.xlsx)** if a person will read it; **CSV (.csv)** if it is going into another system.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the columns">
    **Columns included** names all 27, from **Item name** and **Item type** through **Barcode (UPC/EAN)**, **Reorder level**, **Unit cost**, **Sell price**, **Controlled schedule** and **Track lot & expiry**. No quantity column exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Download it">
    The button reads **Download \{n\} item(s) as Excel**, and the toast confirms **"Exported \{n\} item(s) as Excel."**
  </Step>
</Steps>

Stock movements and valuations come from **Reports** on the same toolbar instead. Sales figures live in [the reports workbench](/pharmacy/money/analytics).

## Who can do this

**Role** decides whether **Inventory** opens at all — a **Cashier / Till Operator** reaches only **POS** and **Sales**, so neither button is theirs. Inside **Inventory**, importing and exporting the catalogue are not separately gated.

| Action | Who by default | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open **Import** and load a catalogue | Everyone who can open **Inventory** | Role — not a grantable permission |
| Open **Export** and download the file | Everyone who can open **Inventory** | Role — not a grantable permission |
| See **Unit cost** and margin on the **Catalog** screen | Pharmacy Manager, Inventory & Purchasing | **View cost prices and margin** |
| Receive the stock these products will hold | Pharmacy Manager, Inventory & Purchasing | **Receive deliveries into stock** |
| Enter quantities in bulk with **Opening stock** | Pharmacy Manager, Inventory & Purchasing | **Adjust stock counts** |

<Warning>
  **The export file carries Unit cost and Sell price for every product**, whether or not the person downloading it can see the margin column on screen. On screen, that figure reads **"Hidden — needs authorisation from your clinic owner."**; in the file it is a plain number. Treat an exported catalogue as commercially sensitive and send it accordingly.
</Warning>

Two behaviours surprise people:

- **Enforcement is opt-in, per person.** A colleague never saved in the permissions sheet is unrestricted, and the sheet warns **"Saving starts enforcing"**.
- **A denied action is recorded; an allowed one is not.** Hiding **Inventory** from a sidebar is a convenience, not a security boundary.

See [Staff permissions](/platform/team/permissions).

## Check it worked

- **After an import:** the new products appear on the **Catalog** tab and are searchable at the till. Their on-hand quantity is zero until you receive stock — that is correct, not a failed import.
- The **Created** and **Updated** tiles match what the preview promised.
- **After an export:** the file opens, the row count matches your catalogue, and the columns match the **Columns included** list.

## Common issues

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="'File exceeds the 5MB limit'">
    Split the file. A catalogue large enough to exceed 5 MB is nearly always several suppliers' lists in one workbook. Import by supplier or category instead — a batch that goes wrong is quick to spot and quick to redo.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'…is not a supported type — use .csv, .xlsx or .xls'">
    The file is something else, or was renamed rather than converted. Open it in Excel or Sheets and save it again as `.csv` or `.xlsx`.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'Could not read that file. Re-save it as .csv or .xlsx and try again.'">
    The file parsed as none of the three formats — usually an unusual encoding from another tool. Re-saving it from a spreadsheet program fixes it in almost every case.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'The file has no data rows below the header.'">
    A header row and nothing under it. Check you have not uploaded the empty template, and that products start on the row directly below the headers with no blank separator row.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'No valid rows to import'">
    Every row failed validation. Compare your headers against the sample CSV — a renamed or missing required column invalidates every row at once. **Item name** and **Item type** are the two that must be there.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'Re-select the file and try again'">
    The file was released before the import ran, usually after a long pause. Choose it again and import straight away.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The preview says 'to update' for products I meant to add">
    Those rows matched an existing barcode or SKU. Either the products are already in your catalogue under different names, or two suppliers share a code. Fix the codes in the spreadsheet before importing, not afterwards.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="'No items to export'">
    Your catalogue is empty. The exporter reads your whole catalogue, so this genuinely means there is nothing there yet.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I import stock levels as well as products?">
    Not through **Import** — there is no quantity column in the file at all, by design. For quantities in bulk, use the **Opening stock** button on the same toolbar, which opens **Opening stock from file**. For everyday arrivals, use **Receive**. That separation is what stops a stale spreadsheet from silently overwriting the shelf.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if I import the same file twice?">
    Nothing bad, as long as your barcodes or SKUs are filled in: the second run matches every row and updates it, leaving the products as they are. Rows with no code to match on are the ones that duplicate.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Will re-importing an export wipe my stock?">
    No. The export has no quantity column to bring back, so a round trip through a spreadsheet cannot change what you hold. It is the safe way to edit prices in bulk.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Excel or CSV — which should I export?">
    Excel for anything a person will read, and if your spreadsheet mangles accents or currency symbols on import. CSV if the file is going into another system. Both carry identical columns.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is there a limit on how many rows I can import at once?">
    The sheet asks for up to 1,000 rows and 5 MB per file. Larger catalogues go in several batches, which is the better habit anyway — one supplier or one category per file.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
