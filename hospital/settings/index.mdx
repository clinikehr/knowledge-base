Settings is where the shape of the hospital is decided — which units exist, who may open what, how money is taken, and what patients see. Each tab has its own page in this section. This one is the map.

<Path steps={['Sidebar', 'Settings']} />

## Getting in, and who can

**Settings** sits in the footer of the main sidebar, below the modules. Selecting it opens the settings area with its own left rail titled **Clinic Settings**. On a phone or a narrow window that rail collapses into a dropdown at the top; the tabs and their order are identical.

You need to be an **owner**, a **manager** or an **accountant**. Anyone else gets: "You don't have permission to access the settings module. Please contact your clinic administrator."

Getting in is not the whole story. Some controls stay **owner-only** even for a manager — **Currency**, **Tax Rate** and **Free Clinic Mode** among them, plus everything on **General Settings** and the inventory **Danger Zone**. Where a card is read-only for you it says so at the bottom: "Only the clinic owner or a manager can change these."

## If a tab you were sent is not there

Settings deep links carry the tab in the address, like `?tab=facility`. If your workspace does not have that tab, the page opens on **General Settings** instead of showing an error. So a link that "does nothing" has almost always landed on General, and the tab genuinely does not exist for this hospital — check the plan, the edition and the care area before you go looking for a bug.

## The five sections

The rail is banded into five sections, and the band headings are the same words on every screen.

| Section | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| **PRACTICE** | The hospital profile, its care areas, departments, wards, theatres, staff, inventory rules and notifications |
| **BILLING** | Subscription and plan, currency and tax, payment processing, memberships, services and the store |
| **PATIENT EXPERIENCE** | Scheduling, the booking page, the contact form, the patient portal and sharable documents |
| **AUTOMATION & AI** | Messaging channels, AI agents and credits |
| **SYSTEM** | Security rules and data export |

One quirk worth knowing once: the first band reads **PRACTICE** and the rail is titled **Clinic Settings** even for a 300-bed hospital. Those words follow the edition, not the plan. Nothing behind them is scaled down.

## What Enterprise has that Solo and Team do not

This is the part worth reading before you go hunting. An Enterprise hospital gets **five tabs no other plan has**, four of them in a rail group called **Departments settings**.

| Tab | What the rail says under it | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Facility** | "Care areas, shifts, nursing & records bands" | Which units this hospital runs, plus the observation, shift, waiting and chase thresholds |
| **Departments** | "Clinic service departments" | Departments, sub-units and consultation rates |
| **Ward Management** | "Inpatient and ward setup" | Wards, sub-wards and bed spaces |
| **Theatre** | "Theatres, safety checklist & enforcement" | Your theatres and the surgical safety checklist rule |
| **Role Navigation** | "What each role sees in the sidebar" | Which modules each role may open, and where they land |

**Theatre needs one more thing.** It appears only once the **Surgical theatre** care area is on, and that area ships **off**. If four of the five are showing and Theatre is not, that is why — turn the area on in [Facility](/hospital/settings/facility) first. **Ward Management** has no care-area switch — Enterprise is all it needs. **Inventory** needs the **Inventory** area.

### And one tab Enterprise loses

**Insurance Settings** is **Solo and Team only**. The two plans run different insurance systems: a hospital's payers, coverage, pre-authorisations and claims live in their own module rather than behind a settings tab. Nothing is missing — it moved. See [Insurance](/hospital/insurance/overview).

Two other tabs never appear here either, and neither is a fault: **Loyalty** belongs to the Pharmacy edition, and **Lab Workflow** to the Diagnostics edition.
