**Clinic Notes** is the hospital's standalone documentation surface: a note about a patient written *without* going through a department queue. Use it for a ward review, a handover, a discharge summary or anything else that is clinical writing rather than an outpatient encounter. Documentation that belongs to an outpatient visit goes on the [consultation](/hospital/outpatient/consultation) chart instead.

## Before you start

**Clinic Notes** has no care-area switch — it is in the sidebar on every plan, and on Enterprise every feature on this page is included. The patient must exist first; register them in [Patients](/hospital/front-desk/patients).

## What you're looking at

The module has its own left rail headed **Clinical Notes**, holding **New Note**, **Templates**, **Custom Templates** and **Snippets**, then an **AI** section with **AI Notes**. **AI Notes** opens a sheet over the page rather than navigating away.

**New Note** opens the **New Progress Note** dashboard, which offers three ways in:

| Card | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Use Template** | "Start from a template" — the structure is already decided |
| **Blank Note** | "Start from scratch" |
| **AI Note Taker** | "AI-powered note generation" — from a recording or a document |

Below those sit three things. **Waiting on you \{n\}** collects notes needing your countersignature — "Notes signed by a colleague you supervise. They are not final until you countersign." A **Quick Note** card lets you write without opening a full editor. And **Recent Notes** has two tabs: **All Notes**, which lists what the hospital has written lately, and **AI Notes Draft**, which lists drafts written by an assistant or through the API and lets you mark each **Reviewed** or move it back to **Needs review** (see [Review a draft the assistant wrote](/practice/clinical/note-assistant#ai-notes-draft)). **AI Notes Draft** has its own **Search drafts...** box, by client, title or type. **All Notes** has **Search notes...**, a **Filter by type** control (**All Types** by default) and columns for **Status** — **Draft** or **Final** — plus **Date**, **Created By** and **Patient**.

A brand-new hospital sees the three entry cards and an empty **Recent Notes** list. Nothing is wrong; nobody has written anything yet.

## Write a quick note

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fill in the Quick Note card">
    Enter a **Note Title** — "Enter note title..." — then choose a **Template Type**: SOAP Note, Progress Note, Initial Assessment, Therapy Session, Discharge Summary, Treatment Plan or General Note.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach it to a patient">
    Use the picker, which is labelled **Client** and searches with "Search clients..." wherever you are on the platform. Choose who the note is about.

    A draft saves without one. A final note does not: you will be told "Please select a client before saving the final note".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add tags and write">
    **Tags** ("Add a tag...") make the note findable later. Write the body in **Note Content** — "Start typing your note content... Type / for snippets", which is how you pull in a saved snippet mid-sentence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save, or sign and lock">
    Saving keeps it as a draft you can come back to — "Draft saved successfully". **Sign & Lock** finishes it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Add the visit details

A saved note has a **Visit details** button in its toolbar, beside the note assistant. A small dot on the button means some details are already recorded. The details record the **Type of visit** (**Ambulatory**, **Emergency**, **Inpatient**, **Virtual** or **Home health**), when the visit **Started** and **Ended**, and a short **Reason for the visit**. These are facts about the visit, not part of what the note says, and none of them is required.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the details">
    On a draft, select **Add visit details**, or **Edit visit details** if some are already there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in what you know">
    Choose the **Type of visit**, pick **Started** and **Ended** from the date and time pickers, and write the **Reason for the visit** in up to 500 characters. Select **Clear start** or **Clear end** to take a time out again. The end cannot be before the start.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Select **Save visit details**. You see "Visit details saved", and they are still there when you leave the note and come back.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A signed note keeps its visit details as they were: its button reads **Visit details** and shows them without letting you change them, with a line saying why. A signed note with no visit details has no button. You can change the details of any draft you are allowed to edit, including one a connected app opened. If you cannot change a colleague's note, you cannot change its visit details either; the message tells you whose note it is.

## Sign and lock

Select **Sign & Lock** to open the **Sign & Lock Note** sheet. Choose the **Signing Provider**, then add the **Provider Signature** — type it or draw it, under the heading **Electronic Signature** — and confirm.

A locked note tells anyone who opens it "This note is locked and cannot be modified" and carries a **Locked** badge. To correct it, select **Unlock Note**, amend, and sign again. Both the lock and the unlock are recorded.

## Only the author changes a note

A note belongs to the person who wrote it. Only that person can change what it says — the editor and autosave are switched off for everyone else, owners and managers included. A colleague's note opens read-only under a banner that says **Written by** their name and the date, with the line "You can read this note and add a follow-up note. Only its author can change what it says."

Owners and managers can still read the note, unlock it and countersign it. What they cannot do is rewrite someone else's words.

If you are shown "This note was written by …" when you try to save, someone else wrote it. Nothing you typed is lost to their note; add your own follow-up note instead.

## Add a follow-up note

Use **Add follow-up note** when you need to add to a note you did not write — or to a note that is already signed. It opens a sheet where you write your entry, sign it (typed or drawn) and confirm. Select **Sign and add**.

Your follow-up note appears **under the original**, with your name, your role and the time you signed it. The original is never edited. Follow-up notes show in the note itself, in the client's chart and in the PDF you export. A doctor who sees a patient return the same day uses a follow-up note rather than reopening the earlier visit.

A follow-up note is part of the record: it cannot be edited or removed afterwards. If you made a mistake in one, add another.

Adding and signing a follow-up note is included on every plan, the Free plan too.

## Countersign a colleague's note

Notes signed by someone you supervise appear under **Waiting on you \{n\}**. Select **Read the note**, read it properly, then countersign. Until you do, the note is not final — the supervising signature is what makes it one.

## Use AI, carefully

Three tools, and they are different things:

- **AI Rewrite** rewrites a passage you have selected.
- **AI Autocomplete** suggests the next few words as you type. Press Tab to accept, or keep typing to ignore it.
- **AI Note Taker** generates a whole note from a recording or an uploaded document, and its sessions are managed from **AI Notes**.
- **Draft with…**, inside the note editor, drafts from text you already have — dictation, a transcript, or your own rough notes.

Both the **Draft with…** button and the **AI Note Taker** use whichever assistant your hospital has chosen — ClinikEHR's own, or an outside one your hospital connects with its own account. See [Choose a note assistant](/practice/clinical/note-assistant).

<Warning>
  **AI text is a first draft, never a record.** Read every sentence before you sign — your signature is what makes the words yours, and a signed note is a legal document about a real patient. Never sign AI output you have not checked against what actually happened.
</Warning>

## Who can do this

| Action | Who by default | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open **Clinic Notes** and write | Any staff member the Outpatient group is shown to | Role Navigation |
| Change a note's words | Only the person who wrote it | Authorship — no permission or role overrides it |
| Add a follow-up note under someone else's note | Anyone who may write notes | The clinic's **Clinical Access** choice for writing a consultation note |
| Sign and lock a note | Anyone who can open a note | Plan — signing is blocked on Free. No permission governs it here; the signing permission belongs to [Consultation](/hospital/outpatient/consultation) |
| Countersign someone else's note | Supervisors | **Countersign a colleague's note** |
| Unlock a signed note | Whoever may sign | Same rule as signing |
| Build a shared note template | Anyone who may manage forms | Forms permissions — new templates are note forms, and existing custom templates are read-only |
| Create and edit snippets | Anyone who can open the page | Plan — creating snippets is blocked on Free |

Two behaviours worth knowing. Permission enforcement is **opt-in per person**: someone never saved in the permissions sheet is unrestricted, and the sheet warns **"Saving starts enforcing"** — so nothing changes for existing staff until they are saved once. And a denied action is recorded in the audit log while an allowed one is not, so hiding **Clinic Notes** in [Role Navigation](/hospital/settings/role-navigation) is a convenience, not a security boundary. See [Permissions](/platform/team/permissions).

## Check it worked

- The note appears under **Recent Notes** with the right patient against it, and on that patient's chart.
- Its **Status** reads **Final** rather than **Draft**.
- A signed note shows the signing provider and refuses edits until unlocked.
- A note you sent for countersignature stays in your supervisor's **Waiting on you \{n\}** list until they act.

## Common issues

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="This note was written on one of your clinic's forms, so it opens in the client's record rather than here">
    You opened a note written on a **note form** from somewhere other than the client's record — for example **View** under **Recent Notes**. Nothing has been lost: every answer is still on the note. Open the client, go to **Notes**, and select the note there to read or continue it. See [Open a note written on a note form](/practice/clinical/note-templates#open-a-note-written-on-a-note-form).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="It will not save as final">
    A final note needs a patient selected. Choose one in the picker and save again. If the patient does not exist yet, register them first in [Patients](/hospital/front-desk/patients).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I opened a colleague's note and I cannot type in it">
    It is read-only because you did not write it. The banner at the top says who did. Select **Add follow-up note** to add your own signed entry under theirs. An owner or manager can read, unlock and countersign it, but cannot rewrite it either.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It says the note is locked and cannot be modified">
    It has been signed. Select **Unlock Note**, make the correction and sign it again — amending a signed note is expected, working around it is not.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My colleague's note is not appearing in Waiting on you">
    Countersignature only reaches the supervisor the note was signed to. Check with the author which provider they picked as the **Signing Provider**, and check your own role can countersign.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Typing '/' does not bring up snippets">
    That shortcut works inside **Note Content** only, and needs at least one saved snippet. Create some under **Snippets** in the left rail.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The picker says 'Client' but we are a hospital">
    The picker keeps that label across every plan. It searches your patient list; the word on the button is the only difference.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I cannot find the note I wrote yesterday">
    Use **Search notes...** and clear **Filter by type**, which persists between visits. **Recent Notes** shows the newest first, so an older note may simply be further down.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="When should I use a clinic note instead of a consultation?">
    A consultation documents an outpatient encounter that came through triage and a department queue, and can carry orders. A clinic note is free-standing documentation about a patient — a ward review, a handover, a discharge summary — with no queue behind it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between Templates and Custom Templates?">
    **Templates** are the ones supplied with the product. **Custom Templates** are the ones your hospital wrote before note forms: they still appear in the picker when you start a note, but they are read-only. A department that wants to standardise its own documentation now builds a **note form** — select **Create a note form** on either page. See [Note templates](/practice/clinical/note-templates).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can two people write the same note?">
    No. One person writes and signs; a supervisor countersigns. A second clinician who has something to add writes their own **follow-up note**, which sits under the original with their name on it. There is no simultaneous editing, which is deliberate — a clinical note has an author, and the signature says who it is.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a manager correct a note a colleague wrote?">
    No. A manager can read it, unlock it so its author can correct it, and countersign it. They can add a follow-up note of their own, but the original wording stays the author's.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does a note appear on the patient's chart automatically?">
    Yes, as soon as it is attached to a patient. A draft appears as a draft; the chart shows the current state rather than waiting for a signature.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Are notes included on our plan?">
    Yes. Every note feature — signing, locking, countersignature, templates, snippets and the AI writing tools — is included on Enterprise. Only the Free plan withholds them.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
