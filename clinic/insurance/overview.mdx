ClinikEHR has **two different insurance systems**, and your plan decides which
one you use. They look similar, they solve the same business problem, and they
share nothing. Read this page before anything else in this section — starting in
the wrong one wastes an afternoon.

## Which one do I have?

| | Enterprise hospitals | Solo and team practices |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | An in-house register you maintain yourself | An electronic clearing-house connection |
| **Where it lives** | **Insurance** in the sidebar | **Insurance** in the sidebar |
| **Claims are** | Prepared by hand and tracked by you | Generated, submitted and tracked electronically |
| **Eligibility is** | Recorded from what the patient tells you | Checked electronically with the payer |
| **Money comes back as** | A payment you record | A remittance the system reconciles |
| **Also needs** | The insurance care area switched on | A payer added and a provider enrolled |

The quickest tell: open **Insurance** in your sidebar. If the first thing you
see is tabs for **Overview**, **Companies**, **Pricing** and **Coverage**, you
are on the hospital system. If it opens on **Unbilled appointments** with a
stats bar reading **Draft / In Progress / Paid / Denied**, you are on the
electronic system.

<Warning>
  The two systems do not share data. Claims raised in one never appear in the
  other, and their analytics are separate reports. If your workspace changes
  plan, the claims you filed before the change stay in the system that created
  them — export anything you need before you switch. See
  [Change your plan](/platform/plans/change-plan).
</Warning>

## The hospital system

<Availability editions={['clinic']} plans="Enterprise" roles="Owners, managers and billers" note="Needs the insurance care area switched on." />

<Path steps={['Sidebar', 'Insurance']} />

Built for hospitals that deal with insurers directly — an HMO, a corporate
scheme, a state programme — and negotiate their own tariffs. It is a register
you maintain: who your insurers are, what you have agreed to charge each of
them, who is covered, and what you have claimed.

The **Insurance Dashboard** has four tabs:

| Tab | What you do there |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | See the shape of your insurance business |
| **Companies** | Add and maintain each insurer |
| **Pricing** | Set payer-specific service and medicine prices |
| **Coverage** | Record which patients are covered, by whom |

Two further pages hang off it: **Pre-Authorization**, for getting approval
before you treat, and **Claims**, for billing after you have.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add your insurers">
    On **Companies**, add each insurer you deal with. Nothing else works until
    at least one exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set what you charge them">
    On **Pricing**, set the negotiated service and medicine prices for that
    payer. These are what claims are priced from — not your standard
    [service list](/clinic/revenue/services).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record patient coverage">
    On **Coverage**, record a patient's insurer, plan and membership details so
    a claim can be raised against them later.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Then: [Request a pre-authorization](/clinic/insurance/pre-authorization) and
[File and track claims](/clinic/insurance/claims).

## The electronic system

<Availability editions={['clinic']} plans="Starter and above; the Free plan is blocked" roles="Owners, managers and billers" />

<Path steps={['Sidebar', 'Insurance']} />

Built for practices billing US payers electronically. It pulls in appointments
you have not billed yet, generates real electronic claims, submits them,
receives remittances back, and checks eligibility with the payer before the
patient arrives.

One panel with four tabs — **Unbilled appointments**, **Claims**, **Payments**
and **Analytics** — above a stats bar showing **Draft**, **In Progress**,
**Paid** and **Denied**.

Setting it up is a three-part job under **Settings → Insurance**:

| Sub-tab | What you do there |
|---|---|
| **Insurance Profile** | Your practice's billing identity |
| **Payers** | Add the payers you bill, from a searchable directory |
| **Enrollment** | Enrol each provider with each payer, per transaction type |

<Note>
  Enrollment is the step people skip, and it is the one that blocks everything.
  A payer will not accept claims, eligibility checks or remittances from a
  provider it has not enrolled — the transaction types are handled separately,
  covering claim status, professional claims, eligibility and remittance.
</Note>

Then: [File and track claims](/clinic/insurance/claims).

<Note>
  Eligibility checks and claim submissions are charged per use on this system,
  and what you pay depends on your plan — the Team plan includes a monthly
  allowance of claims before per-claim charges begin. See
  [Plans](/platform/plans/overview).
</Note>

## What both systems assume

- **The patient's coverage is recorded.** Neither system can bill a payer it
  does not know the patient has.
- **Your services carry codes.** A **Procedure Code** on a
  [service](/clinic/revenue/services) carries through to claims.
- **Insurance sits next to ordinary billing, not instead of it.** Patient
  responsibility — co-pays, excess, uncovered items — is still an
  [invoice](/clinic/revenue/invoices).

## If Insurance is missing from your sidebar

- On the Free plan the electronic system is blocked; the upgrade prompt names
  **Starter**.
- On Enterprise, the hospital system needs the insurance
  [care area](/platform/team/care-areas) switched on. Turning that off removes
  the nav group, the settings tab and the analytics report together.
- If the group is there but you cannot open it, it is your role and
  permissions — see [Permissions](/platform/team/permissions).
